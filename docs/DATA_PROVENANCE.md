# Data provenance and privacy

## Source and reuse

Olist and its collaborators released the **Brazilian E-Commerce Public Dataset by Olist** on Kaggle. It contains historical marketplace transactions in Brazil from 2016 to 2018. Olist describes the records as anonymized commercial data. The source contained linkable customer identifiers, locations, and review text, so this project limits ingestion to fields needed for the analysis. [Olist’s dataset card](https://www.kaggle.com/olistbr/brazilian-ecommerce) · [Readable source description](https://www.kaggle.com/olistbr/brazilian-ecommerce/home/)

Kaggle lists the dataset as **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**. This project uses it for local educational analysis. The license requires attribution and a link to the license when sharing licensed material, restricts commercial use, and applies ShareAlike to adaptations when shared. Document the transformations below in any reuse and verify that the intended distribution complies with the license. [Creative Commons deed](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/) · [License text](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/legalcode.en)

Acquire version **2** from Kaggle. The runner loads the selected columns into an in-memory SQLite database and writes aggregate reports and a manifest to the requested output folder. The manifest records each input file’s SHA-256 and row count, the archive SHA-256 if using the ZIP, and aggregate report checksums. The included source lock records the fingerprints of the seven inputs from the official archive. Keep the archive and any extracts in local working storage. Separate synthetic inputs are generated by the offline fixture for testing. ## Fields processed

| Source file | Allowlisted source columns | Analytical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `olist_customers_dataset.csv` | `customer_id`, `customer_unique_id`, `customer_state` | Join the order’s customer record, group repeat purchases under the source’s persistent customer key, and summarize delivery by state. |
| `olist_orders_dataset.csv` | `order_id`, `customer_id`, `order_status`, `order_purchase_timestamp`, `order_delivered_customer_date`, `order_estimated_delivery_date` | Assign order month and outcome, and compare estimated with actual delivery. |
| `olist_order_items_dataset.csv` | `order_id`, `order_item_id`, `product_id`, `seller_id`, `price`, `freight_value` | Calculate merchandise and freight, and assign items to product categories and sellers. |
| `olist_order_payments_dataset.csv` | `order_id`, `payment_sequential`, `payment_value` | Reconcile recorded payments without duplicating order totals. |
| `olist_products_dataset.csv` | `product_id`, `product_category_name` | Classify items without descriptive text or product attributes. |
| `olist_sellers_dataset.csv` | `seller_id` | Validate that each item references a known seller. |
| `product_category_name_translation.csv` | `product_category_name`, `product_category_name_english` | Provide readable category names. |

The ingestion projects the allowed columns, converts monetary values to integer BRL cents, and stores the estimate at calendar-day precision. Report logic uses purchase months and delivery date differences. This preserves the day-level meaning of the source delivery estimate and avoids interpreting midnight on that date as a cutoff.

## Exposure and analytical limits

- Review files, free text, city, ZIP prefixes, and coordinates are outside the analytical model. Approval, carrier handoff, and shipping-limit timestamps, payment methods and seller locations are also excluded.
- Order, customer, product, and seller keys support internal joins and grouping. Reports export aggregate rows without these keys or exact transaction timestamps. A state needs at least 20 delivered orders to appear. Its on-time and duration metrics each need at least 20 orders in their own eligible sample. Seller and product volume tiers require at least 20 sellers or products respectively. Categories with fewer than 20 distinct delivered orders are grouped and shown only if the combined group reaches 20 distinct orders. Payments are grouped across delivered and other statuses.
- For month-over-month and cohort comparisons, January 2017 through August 2018 is the study window. The 2016 extract is sparse. September 2018 contains 16 purchased orders and October contains four, with zero delivered orders across both months. Monthly counts retain flagged months outside the window and omit their monetary values and growth rates. Earlier delivered history still informs first-purchase identification and historical totals. Customer follow-up ends on August 31, 2018. These bounds are analytical coverage decisions based on the extract’s observed pattern.
- The persistent customer key identifies an entity as represented by the source. Repeat behavior can be observed only within the recorded orders and date range.
- Merchandise sales are measured before any refund or cost adjustment. Freight and recorded payments have separate definitions. The source supplies no verified cost of goods sold or fee schedule for this analysis, so contribution scenarios use disclosed assumptions.
- Aggregation and support floors reduce exposure. Combining reports with external data can still reveal information about source records.
